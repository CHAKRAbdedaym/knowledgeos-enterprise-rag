## Lifecycle gates

The lifecycle consists of qualification, discovery, implementation, acceptance, stabilization and closure. Qualification establishes a business owner, need, capacity and constraints. The standard Discovery offer lasts two weeks and produces priority journeys, a backlog, initial architecture and risks. Its exit gate checks whether success criteria are understandable and an implementation decision can be made. Major uncertainty remains visible instead of being turned into a firm estimate simply because a commercial calendar requires an answer.

## Prepare implementation

Before work starts, define environments, access, controls and communication. The priority backlog has testable acceptance criteria. Customer dependencies have an owner and expected date. Demonstration data is synthetic. Delivery checks capacity for the two-week iteration; Platform reviews operation and security. Structural decisions are documented for later handover. A risk that changes the delivery plan must reach the business owner while alternatives still exist, rather than appearing for the first time at final acceptance.

## Increments and acceptance

Every iteration targets an executable, demonstrable outcome. Reviews show journeys, errors and limitations. Done requires review, checks and useful documentation. Scope changes are arbitrated with effort and schedule consequences. The business owner joins intermediate verification so final acceptance is not the first contact with the product. Acceptance uses agreed scenarios and distinguishes defects, enhancements and new requests. A defect includes reproduction, expected result, observed result and severity. The business owner records acceptance or reservations in writing.

## Stabilize and close

Standard stabilization lasts ten working days after acceptance and covers defects in accepted scope. New behavior is not automatically a defect. Recurring support needs a separate agreement. Closure requires accessible code and documentation, an operational owner, completed training and known issues. Temporary access is revoked and secrets transferred securely. The record includes acceptance, limitations and version references. Project records are reviewed 24 months after closure under fictional retention policy. Administrative closure does not erase actual contractual duties or authorized evidence requirements.

## Applying and maintaining this guidance

Before acting, verify the scope, version and responsible role. Record an exception with its rationale, owner, expiration and next review. Report a contradiction to the document owner rather than silently combining incompatible instructions. A useful request states the expected result, observed situation and decision needed, without including secrets. Keep approved decisions in the relevant project or restricted administrative record. To revise this note, propose a reviewed change and update related references. These examples support retrieval experiments and portfolio interviews; they do not provide evidence of an actual deployment, real customer outcome or operating certification.

## Worked example

Acceptance identifies a missing behavior that was never in agreed scope. The team distinguishes a new request from a defect, records the business decision and preserves the existing acceptance evidence. Stabilization does not silently become an unlimited development phase.
