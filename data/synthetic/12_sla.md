## Coverage window

The fictional support agreement covers Monday through Friday, 09:00–18:00 Paris time, excluding French public holidays. Continuous 24/7 support is not included by default. Customers open tickets through the contractually agreed channel. A response clock starts when the ticket is received inside the coverage window. Tickets received outside that window begin counting at the next opening unless an explicit option provides different coverage. A calendar day and a covered working day are therefore different measurement units.

## First response targets

The first response target is 30 business minutes for SEV1, two business hours for SEV2 and one working day for SEV3. A first response means a person acknowledges the ticket, qualifies initial impact and states the next step. It does not mean the service has been repaired. The coordinator confirms severity with the customer and explains changes based on observed impact. An automatic message alone is not the intended human qualification represented by these fictional targets.

## Restoration targets

Internal restoration targets are four business hours for SEV1 and one working day for SEV2. SEV3 is scheduled by agreement. A stable workaround can count as restoration before the final fix. These are demonstration targets, not universal commercial guarantees. Missing access, customer decisions or external dependencies may block progress and must be visible with an owner. The team communicates uncertainty instead of converting an internal target into a promised resolution timestamp unsupported by current evidence.

## Availability and reporting

The fictional monthly availability objective is 99.5% for the covered service. Measurement uses the contractual service window and eligible interruptions. Planned maintenance is announced five working days ahead and treated according to the signed agreement. Exclusions and service credits are not defined here and must not be invented. SEV1 status updates occur every 30 minutes during handling. Monthly review considers volumes, response performance and recurring incidents. A SEV1 or SEV2 review occurs within five working days after stabilization, following the incident procedure.

## Applying and maintaining this guidance

Before acting, verify the scope, version and responsible role. Record an exception with its rationale, owner, expiration and next review. Report a contradiction to the document owner rather than silently combining incompatible instructions. A useful request states the expected result, observed situation and decision needed, without including secrets. Keep approved decisions in the relevant project or restricted administrative record. To revise this note, propose a reviewed change and update related references. These examples support retrieval experiments and portfolio interviews; they do not provide evidence of an actual deployment, real customer outcome or operating certification.

## Worked example

A SEV1 ticket arrives shortly before the coverage window closes. The coordinator uses covered business time for target accounting, explains the agreed coverage and records the next step. No one silently converts the agreement into continuous overnight support.
