## Cadence

Implementation projects use two-week iterations. The business owner owns value and priority; the Delivery lead coordinates capacity and risks. Planning selects a coherent goal and a small set of verifiable outcomes. A daily coordination meeting lasts no more than fifteen minutes. The review demonstrates executable behavior. The retrospective selects an improvement whose effect can be examined in the next iteration. Friday demos may show exploration, provided its prototype status and remaining uncertainty remain visible to participants.

## Ready backlog

A request states user context, expected outcome and observable acceptance criteria. Dependencies and data constraints are identified before commitment. An estimate expresses magnitude and uncertainty, not a commercial guarantee. Large requests are divided by usable value. Priority combines business value, risk, genuine urgency and effort. Blocked items retain a resolution owner and next review date. A board entry with no clear next action should trigger clarification instead of accumulating indefinitely as apparently active work.

## Definition of done

Done means reviewed code, appropriate automated checks, functional verification and useful documentation updates. Migrations and configuration changes are included in preparation. Technical completion and business acceptance remain distinct states. Known defects are declared. The main branch remains runnable to support controlled delivery. A hidden feature can be technically ready without being accepted. A successful demonstration does not replace the agreed acceptance scenarios or erase known limitations merely because the audience liked the presentation.

## Risk and measurement

The risk register contains probability, impact, early signal, response and owner. It is reviewed every Thursday for twenty minutes. Scope changes state effort and date consequences; the business owner arbitrates. Useful indicators include lead time, blocked work, escaped defects and acceptance feedback. They improve the work system rather than ranking individuals by commits or ticket volume. Weekly reporting states results, risks and decisions required. Reduced capacity leads to an explicit scope decision instead of an invisible expectation of permanent overtime.

## Applying and maintaining this guidance

Before acting, verify the scope, version and responsible role. Record an exception with its rationale, owner, expiration and next review. Report a contradiction to the document owner rather than silently combining incompatible instructions. A useful request states the expected result, observed situation and decision needed, without including secrets. Keep approved decisions in the relevant project or restricted administrative record. To revise this note, propose a reviewed change and update related references. These examples support retrieval experiments and portfolio interviews; they do not provide evidence of an actual deployment, real customer outcome or operating certification.

## Worked example

A new urgent request arrives mid-iteration. The business owner compares its value and risk with committed work, chooses a substitution and records the schedule effect. The team updates the board so stakeholders can see the actual agreement.
